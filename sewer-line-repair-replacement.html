Warning signs of a sewer line problem

Inside the house

  • Multiple drains slow or backing up at once
  • Sewage smell in the basement or yard
  • Toilets bubbling when the washer drains
  • Backups that return weeks after a cleaning

Outside the house

  • A strip of grass that is greener than the rest of the lawn
  • Soft, sunken, or soggy spots over the sewer line path
  • Older home with original clay or cast iron pipe
  • Big trees between the house and the street

Sewer line questions

Is the sewer line my responsibility or the township's?

In most of our area, the line from the house to the street connection (the lateral) is the homeowner's responsibility. Your plumber can tell you where your line meets the public main, and township rules vary.

Can roots be cleared without replacing the line?

Often, yes. Roots can be cut and the line jetted, which buys time. If roots keep returning, it means the pipe has openings roots can enter, and a camera inspection will show whether repair or lining is the smarter money.

What is trenchless replacement?

Methods that replace or reline the pipe underground with little or no digging along its path. Not every line qualifies (a fully collapsed pipe may not), which is what the camera inspection decides.
