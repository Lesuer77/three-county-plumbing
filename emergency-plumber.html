Do these three things while you call

1

Shut off the water

The main shutoff is usually near the water meter or where the line enters the house. Turning it off stops the damage clock.

2

Kill the power if water is near it

If water is pooling near outlets, the panel, or appliances, switch off the breaker for that area. Do not stand in water to do it.

3

Move what you can, then call us

Boxes, rugs, and electronics off the floor. Then call and describe what is happening. We route emergency calls first.

What counts as an emergency

Emergency: call now, any hour

  • Burst or frozen pipe
  • Active leak you cannot stop
  • Sewer backing up into the home
  • No water in the whole house
  • Water heater leaking heavily
  • Gas smell near a water heater (leave the house and call 911 first)

Urgent, but can be scheduled

  • A single slow or clogged drain
  • Dripping faucet or running toilet
  • Low water pressure
  • Water heater making noise but working
  • Planning a replacement before failure

Not sure which one yours is? Call anyway. We will help you judge it in a minute.

Emergency questions

Do emergency calls cost more?

Many plumbers charge a higher rate for after-hours work, and your plumber will quote the price before starting, including any emergency fee. What we can tell you: being connected through us costs you nothing, and you always hear the price before the work.

How fast will someone arrive?

It depends on who is on call in your county and how many emergencies are running. Your call goes live to a local company, not a dispatch queue in another state, and they will give you their honest arrival window on the call.

A pipe burst and my basement is flooding. Who pays for the damage?

Stop the water first, then your plumber stops the problem. For the damage itself, document everything with photos and call your homeowners insurance. Your plumber's invoice and notes help that claim.
